# E063B — CLOSED-only direct constructive attempt via 2-MOLS trade connectivity

**Research date:** 2026-11-03. **Problem status: OPEN.** No 100/100/100 witness, and no unrestricted nonexistence proof. MOLS10 hourly task stays disabled.

## Goal-backward direct construction target

For an orthogonal pair (A,B) of order 10, a set of **eight disjoint common transversals with bipartite 20-cell residual conflict graph** immediately gives the tenth/last two transversals and hence the third orthogonal Latin square. This is an exact constructive closure gate. A large number of common transversals *without disjointness* is not progress.

## New general exact trade criterion

Let A and B be orthogonal Latin squares of order n, choose distinct symbols x,y in A, and consider swapping x and y in a nonempty subset of the 2n cells where A is x or y, while leaving B unchanged. Define a graph G(A,B;x,y) on these 2n cells, joining two cells if they have the same (1) row, (2) column, or (3) B-symbol.

**Lemma (elementary).** The resulting square A' is Latin *and* remains orthogonal to B **if and only if** the swapped subset is a union of entire connected components of G.

**Proof:** Each row has exactly one x-cell and one y-cell. Swapping only one would duplicate one of the symbols and omit the other, so Latin row uniqueness requires exchanging both or neither. Exactly the same argument applies independently to columns. Moreover, orthogonality to B requires each of the original (x,b),(y,b) pairs to remain exactly once, for every symbol b of B; therefore the two cells with B-symbol b and A-symbols x/y must also be swapped together or not at all. These three conditions are exactly closure under all edges of G. Since no other A-symbols are affected, they are also sufficient. The analogous statement holds for a two-symbol exchange in B with A fixed. This is a structural encoding of known Latin trades; no claim of discovery of a new published theorem.

This gives a mathematically **sound operation that changes an orthogonal pair without losing orthogonality** whenever G is disconnected. Crucially, this is stronger than merely preserving one Latin square.

## Executed results: joint A/B two-symbol trade closures

We next permit A and B to change simultaneously, where each intermediate matrix is a **valid Latin square** (but intermediates need not be orthogonal). Start from the Egan A/B pair with disjoint packing7.

- One trade in each matrix, including one unchanged side: **9,558** pair candidates, 0 valid changed orthogonal pairs.
- At most two sequential trades in A and at most one in B: **726,168** candidate combinations; at most two in B and at most one in A: **583,704**. These two families together yield 63 orthogonal pair hits, but every hit is an exact whole-symbol relabel of the original pair (36 distinct A relabels and 27 B relabels considered separately). None is genuinely new.
- **Two sequential Latin two-symbol cycle trades in EACH square:** A has 9,192 distinct non-original two-trade matrices; B has 4,824; full Cartesian product **44,342,208 pairs** exhaustively checked in optimized C++ in ~1.1 s. Exactly **972** pairs remain orthogonal. Independent Python verified all 972 ordered-pair overlays and proved each is a global A-symbol relabel combined with a global B-symbol relabel: 36×27=972, with **zero non-relabel cases**. As such they still have exactly 14 common transversals, maximum disjoint packing7. No 8-packing or full solution.

**Correct scope:** all variants generated by at most two sequential two-symbol component trades in EACH member of THIS particular Egan A/B pair (plus separately A/C's own orthogonal-trade-connected component). This does **not** rule out more complex trades, other pairs, or the unrestricted 3-MOLS(10) problem. There is no global closure and no credible ETA.

## Theory-level audit: why the Egan seed cannot be the answer

McKay, Meynert and Myrvold, *Small Latin Squares, Quasigroups and Loops*, Journal of Combinatorial Designs 15 (2007), 98–119, DOI https://doi.org/10.1002/jcd.20105, computationally established that **no order-10 Latin square having a nontrivial autoparatopism group can be a member of a full triple of 3-MOLS(10)**. Their theorem must be distinguished from a weaker prohibition on automorphisms of the triple *as a whole*. This is a known peer-reviewed result, not claimed as a new theorem.

An explicit nonidentity permutation `p=[0,2,3,1,5,6,4,8,9,7]` satisfies `S[p[r]][p[c]]==p[S[r][c]]` in **all 100 cells of each of the three Egan–Wanless near-triple squares**, checked independently in JavaScript. Therefore neither A, B nor C from this source may occur, up to paratopism, in an unrestricted order-10 triple. In particular the 972 exact surviving global relabelings of Egan A/B are excluded from closure **by the preexisting theorem**, whether or not their disjoint packings were computed. This is a strategically important correction to earlier E059–E063 attempts: Egan's highly symmetric seed is a poor candidate for a real CLOSED solution; those exercises were useful for testing methods, but cannot on their own generate the desired triple without replacing the symmetric squares.

An independent E063 investigation on the same Drive/GitHub workspace is already examining 216 fixed seven-C-clue Latin completions, 14 orbits and symmetry exclusions. E063B is deliberately a separate trade-neighborhood study and does not overwrite it. No unrestricted symmetry classes were closed here.

## Research decision

The disjoint-transversal criterion still directly leads to CLOSED; the Egan pair's small Latin cycle-trade neighborhoods are now **ruled out as useful construction routes**. Future closure attempts should NOT spend resources on the same neighborhood or a random corpus dominated by zero-transversal pairs. A logically stronger target is a genuinely different 2-MOLS pair family that can support 8 disjoint common transversals, or complete unrestricted five-coordinate OA proof-producing SAT. Record `UNKNOWN` whenever exhaustive global coverage is absent.
