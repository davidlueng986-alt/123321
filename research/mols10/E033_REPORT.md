## Globally valid normalization lemma
Normalize first row of A,B,C independently to identity, then reorder rows so A[r,0]=r. The second row f(c)=A[1,c] is a derangement with f(0)=1. Any simultaneous column permutation p and common symbol relabeling p^{-1} transforms S'[r,c]=p^{-1}(S[r,p(c)]), preserving Latin and all pairwise overlays and conjugating f to p^{-1}fp. Choose p to label cycles of f consecutively with the first largest cycle starting at 0,1. Reorder rows by the first column of A; A row1 remains the canonical f because f(0)=1. Thus every valid 3-MOLS(n) admits one of the canonical cycle-type second rows. For n=10 there are **12** derangement cycle types versus **148,329** possible normalized second rows (D(10)/9). This is complete orbit coverage, not an existence claim.

## Second-row B centralizer action
For fixed canonical f, any second row g of B is a permutation avoiding g(i)=i and g(i)=f(i). The centralizer C(f), generated by cycle rotations and swaps of equal-length cycles, acts on g by conjugation. One g per C(f)-orbit suffices for a possible completion. Two **independent exact** algorithms agree on all 12 counts: (1) enumerate every g and traverse generator orbits; (2) enumerate C(f) and apply Burnside, counting each fixed g via p-cycle matching with exact permanent DP (no g-orbit enumeration).
