# CAP-004D — The index-two order-60 subgroup does not break orbit symmetry
**Date:** 2026-11-01 UTC. **Scope:** the specific Paley signed-permutation action on the six trailing coordinates of F3^7. **Global r3(7): OPEN.**

## Starting point and tested hypotheses
CAP-004B: full order-240 invariance has restricted optimum 225. CAP-004C: canonical order-120 even-permutation invariance has restricted optimum 236 (independent direct cap checker; CI https://github.com/davidlueng986-alt/123321/actions/runs/41344134381). The CAP-004D backlog proposed groups of orders 60, 40, 24. Before an expensive cap search, determine which groups actually exist and whether they change the six-dimensional orbit partition.

## Exact arithmetic certificate
Script: `python research/capset_f3_7/c6_subgroup_lattice_certificate.py` (stdlib; no random seed, GPU, or solver). It regenerates the verified 112-point Paley-basis cap, enumerates the 240 signed coordinate permutations preserving it, and selects the 120 even-permutation elements `E`. It constructs the full 120x120 group multiplication table and exact commutators.

1. `|E|=120`; its derived subgroup `D=[E,E]` has **60** elements. `[D,D]=D` (perfect). The center `Z(E)` has **2** elements, and `D ∩ Z(E)={1}`. Thus `E=D × C2`. This is an explicitly checked finite-group structure, not a literature novelty claim.
2. **No order-40 subgroup exists in E.** Such a subgroup would have index 3, giving a transitive coset action `E -> S3`. The perfect subgroup `D` has trivial image in `S3`, so the image factors through `E/D ≅ C2`, which cannot act transitively on three cosets. This is a short mathematical deduction from the exact group certificate.
3. **Order 60 exists**, namely `D`. However, the complete partitions of all **729 points of F3^6** into `D`-orbits and `E`-orbits are **identical**, verified by comparing the full orbit sets, not just orbit sizes. Both have 21 orbits; 60-orbit sizes: 1×1, 12×4, 20×4, 30×4, 60×8. The order-60 internal orbit line constraints have 732 distinct forbidden masks, 174 cap-valid slice masks, and maximum single-slice size 112 in an independent scratch enumeration.
4. Consequently the families of F3^7 caps invariant under `D` and `E` (acting identically on all three six-dimensional slices) are literally the **same family**. The already certified CAP-004C result transfers: **order-60 invariant restricted optimum = 236**. This does **not** establish an unrestricted upper bound.
5. **Order 24 exists:** find an order-12 subgroup generated by order-3 and order-2 elements of `D`, then adjoin the nontrivial central element. One deterministic such group has **45 orbits** on F3^6: 1×1, 6×4, 8×4, 12×16, 24×20. Of its 45 individual orbits, 33 are themselves cap-valid. A direct `2^45` subset-max DP would be infeasible without substantial structural pruning. No order-24 optimum has been computed.

## Independent scratch check and audit
A separate standalone pure-Python reconstruction (re-deriving the classical 112-point slice from the ten design blocks, instead of importing the repository generator) produced the same group structure, identical 60/120 orbit partitions, and 45 order-24 orbits. This scratch run was CPU-only and deterministic; its transient file was `/mnt/data/capset_subgroup_probe.py`, not a durable repository artifact. The committed certificate is the durable replay. The workflow now executes it alongside the existing independent 236-point direct verifier and the CAP-004C exhaustive restricted optimum.

**Falsified planning assumption:** orders 60, 40 and 24 are not three equally promising symmetry-breaking branches. Order 40 is impossible inside this E; order 60 exists but does not change orbit-invariant search space; only order 24 among these three creates a larger family. This excludes **these subgroup-invariant models only**.
